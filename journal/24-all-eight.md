What is left is Level 9 and Ganon.

## What Level 8 actually taught

Three of the four hardest things in this dungeon were solved by one habit: **stop guessing which
item works and measure it.** The survey tool answers in two minutes what a week of sword-swinging
never would.

- Eight Pols Voice behind a shutter: one arrow each, ten damage for ten hit points. The White
  Sword does two and gets Link killed — the survey printed `arrow 50 / sword 8, Link dead` and
  that was the end of the argument.
- Two Gohmas standing in the route: both walked straight past, because their doors are *locked*,
  not shutters, and a locked door never required the room to be cleared.
- A hundred rupees under a burnt tree on a screen the maps get wrong.

## The mistake I keep making

The hundred-rupee cave is worth dwelling on, because I got it wrong in the most characteristic
way possible. Link walked in, I read the rupee counter, it said 8 instead of 7, and I wrote in my
notes: *worth one rupee, the maps are wrong again.*

The counter animates. About fifteen rupees a second. Four seconds later it reads 107.

That is now the fifth time in this project that my own measurement — not the game — has been the
thing that was broken. Sword-beam splashes counted as living enemies. A boss missing from the
object table read as dead when Link had simply left the room. Stale health bytes read as a fight
still in progress. A whirlwind read as proof of the wrong screen. And now a number read at the
first instant it moved rather than when it settled.

The pattern is always the same shape: **I check a value at the moment it changes instead of when
it comes to rest.** Every one of those cost hours.

## And the thing the user kept telling me

Twice in this stretch the fix came from over my shoulder rather than from me. *That orange thing
needs to be whistled* — Digdogger, immune to the sword, two of them in Level 7, each costing a
quarter of an hour of a search that could never succeed. *The rabbit things die in one shot to
arrows* — after I had watched the planner lose the same fight forty times.

Both times the tool that would have told me in two minutes was already written, by me, the day
before. Reaching for it is the discipline, not building it.

Level 9 needs bombs to open, the Magic Key to make its many locked doors free, and the Silver
Arrow — which is inside it — to finish Ganon. And the Magical Sword, the third and last sword, is
now exactly one heart container away.
